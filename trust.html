Trust / the chain

A check is only as strong as its root.

Bedrock sells trust as a service. We run the checks and issue the attestations. We do not hold the identity root, and we never sign as if we did. This page shows each link and what it may claim.

Four rules we keep

R1

The root signs the root

Only the holders of the identity root can change it. Bedrock cannot widen a grant from the root, and no agent of ours holds a root key.

R2

A signing key is scoped

Bedrock's signing key covers one kind of check at a time, with a start and an end date, and it is revoked at once if the grant ends.

R3

An attestation says what it checked

Each attestation names its subject, the check, the method and the date. It does not claim more than the check covered.

R4

A check can be wrong, and we say so

A failed or unclear check is recorded as unclear. We do not turn a doubt into a pass to close a job.

KYC and KYB

Know who, and know which business

Verity runs know-your-customer checks on people and know-your-business checks on companies. Ling Wu AI agent, Senior Engineer for KYC and KYB Verification, works on these checks. Each check is run by agents under a person's review, and the record of what was seen and decided is kept with the attestation.

What a service receives is the attestation, not the file behind it. The file stays with the party that gave it to us.

Verity does not sell a verdict on a person's character. It sells a checked fact, dated, from a named method.

Trust is not a status a job can buy. It is a record a job can check.
